Preference elicitation

Teach a movie recommender what you like

This project compares two simple ways of telling a new movie recommender about your taste: choosing between two movies, and ranking ten movies from favourite to least favourite.

What will happen? You will try both methods, answer a few short questions about how they felt, and then make several final movie choices so the two learned models can be compared fairly.

Assignment checklist

How every required task is completed

Open any section below for a non-technical explanation. The PDF report gives the full academic justification and study protocol.

Task 1Choose and extract movie featuresHow a movie becomes information the model can learn fromCompleted

Each movie is converted into a small set of understandable characteristics: genres, release year, duration, IMDb score, whether the language is English, and content rating. Genres and ratings become yes/no indicators, while numerical values are scaled so one field does not dominate only because its numbers are larger.

Example: a recent English-language Action movie can be represented as “Action = yes, Comedy = no, newer year = positive value, English = yes,” plus the other fields.

Why this choice? The user gives only a limited number of answers, so a compact and interpretable feature set is easier to learn reliably than thousands of actor or director identity fields.

Task 2Extend Bradley–Terry to a full rankingHow the model learns from a list of ten moviesCompleted

For two movies, Bradley–Terry says the movie with the higher hidden preference score is more likely to be chosen. For a ranking, this project uses the Plackett–Luce extension: treat the ranking as a sequence of choices.

  1. Choose the favourite from all 10 movies.
  2. Choose the favourite from the 9 remaining movies.
  3. Continue until the complete order is explained.

This is consistent with Bradley–Terry because when only two movies remain, it becomes the same two-item probability model.

Task 3Design the complete user studyHypotheses, participants, procedure, measures, and analysisCompleted

The proposed study uses a within-subject, counterbalanced design: every participant tries both methods, while the starting method is randomly changed between participants. This makes the comparison fairer because each person acts as their own comparison.

The plan includes recruitment and eligibility, informed consent, randomized method order, both elicitation methods, ease/confidence questions, held-out validation choices, completion-time measurement, analysis, privacy, and debriefing. The study is designed only; the assignment does not require recruiting participants.

Two-movie choice

Ten-movie ranking

Arrange ten movies from most preferred to least preferred. One ranking communicates many relative preferences.

Fair comparison

Separate final choices are not used for learning. They are kept aside to check which learned model better predicts your preferences.

Helpful terms in simple words

What is a “preference”?

Your personal choice. For example, if you would rather watch Movie A than Movie B, the study treats that as evidence that A is preferred to B for you.

What is “preference elicitation”?

It simply means asking questions that help a system learn what a person likes.

What is a “feature”?

A feature is one piece of movie information used by the model, such as genre, release year, duration, language, or content rating.

What is the “utility score”?

It is an internal numerical score produced by the model from movie features and estimated user preferences. A higher score means the model believes the user is more likely to prefer that movie.

What is Bradley–Terry?

It is a mathematical model for choices between two items. It turns the difference between their utility scores into the probability that one will be chosen over the other.

What is Plackett–Luce?

It extends the same choice idea to a whole ranking by repeatedly selecting the most preferred movie from those still remaining.

What does “held-out” mean?

Held-out choices are saved until the end and are not used to train either model. They act like a small test so both methods can be evaluated on the same unseen decisions.
